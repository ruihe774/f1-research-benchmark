(4) (91 FR 54791): "To the extent that implementation of this proclamation requires a refund of duties collected, refunds shall be processed pursuant to applicable law and CBP's standard procedures for such refunds." - **C** (arrives 11:30 p.m. Sept. 28; entry 9:00 a.m. Sept. 29): imported before Sept. 29 → not banned; it was "imported, but not yet entered for consumption … prior to September 29, 2026," so cl. (2) applies → **$15,000**.
- **D** (arrives 12:30 a.m. Sept. 29): imported on/after the effective time → **excluded from importation**; no consumption entry may be made. No section 338 duty arises because there is no lawful importation (the duty is replaced by the ban).

(b) **E** (arrives Sept. 25; admitted to an FTZ Sept. 25; entry for consumption from the zone Oct. 6): because it was admitted to the zone after the Aug. 22 effective date, 11046 cl. (4) (91 FR 46641) requires admission in "privileged foreign status" (unless domestic status applies), and on entry for consumption it is subject to the ad valorem rate for its heading. On the exclusion side, E was imported before Sept. 29, so 11061 cl. (2) applies: it "will remain subject to the 50 percent duty rate" → **50% × $30,000 = $15,000**, and no import ban.

(c) If a court invalidates the import ban as applied to this beer, 11061 cl. (9)(b) (91 FR 58313) provides that "the 50 percent ad valorem duty imposed in Proclamation 11046 shall apply to the import to which the invalidated import ban … had applied before its invalidation." So later shipments of the beer would be importable and dutiable at **50%** under Proclamation 11046.

(d) No. 11061 cl. (3) (91 FR 58312) states that, besides converting listed products from duty to ban, "this proclamation does not otherwise affect the products, as further established in the Proclamation of September 8, 2026 (Modifying the Scope … With Respect to Alcoholic Beverages), that are subject to the additional ad valorem duties imposed in Proclamation 11046." Proc. 11061 cl. (1), (2), (3), (9)(b) (2026-18835, 91 FR 58312–58313); Proc. 11046 cl. (1), (4) (2026-14991, 91 FR 46641); Proc. 11064 cl. (1) (2026-18838, 91 FR 58332).

**Uncertainty:**
The documents nowhere address the treatment of goods held in a foreign trade zone when the ban's effective date passes; the outcome in (b) rests on 11061 cl. For (d) the text is clear; for (c) the text does not say whether the 50% duty applies retroactively to goods denied entry while the ban was in force.

- **Section 301:** not in force before July 24, 2026; from then, exempt because the goods are entered under 9903.82.02, a heading listed in note 52(f)(1) (91 FR 47362); in Case U also exempt under note 52(g).
- **Section 338:** not in force before Aug. 22, 2026; from Aug. 22 to Sept. 14, 2026 barred by 11048 cl. (2)'s section 232 carve-out (91 FR 46665); owed from Sept. 15, 2026.
- **10% section 122 surcharge:** never owed — Feb. 24 to July 24, 2026 only (Proc. 11012, 91 FR 9339), and exempt as a 232-subject article (recital 14(k), 91 FR 9341).
- **Reciprocal IEEPA tariff (EO 14257):** its rates did not apply in addition to the northern-border duties (§(e), 90 FR 15046) and the order's IEEPA duties were terminated by EO 14389.

**Uncertainty:**
The 232 aluminum rate for the Feb. 10, 2025 entry (10%) is taken from Proclamation 10895's recital that the prior rate was 10% and its clause (2) raising it to 25% on Mar. 12, 2025; no separate 10% rate clause was re-read. The surcharge's 232 exemption is stated in recital 14(k) with reference to image annexes; the expiry date (July 24, 2026) is computed from "150 days … effective February 24, 2026" because no termination or extension notice appears in the Federal Register. Whether the 12% contingent rate in EO 14257 §(e) ever became operative is not established by any published document, and in any event the order imposing it was terminated by EO 14389.
