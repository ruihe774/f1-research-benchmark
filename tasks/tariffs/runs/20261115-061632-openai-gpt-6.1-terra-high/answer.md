(b) **Why the other layers are zero:**
- **Northern-border IEEPA:** paused on February 10, 2025. It was originally scheduled at 25% for March 6 and for non-USMCA March 10 entries, and later 35% for non-USMCA goods, but CBP reports that the Supreme Court held the IEEPA tariffs unlawful. Thus this table does not treat them as legally owed today. U was expressly excepted from March 7, 2025; from June 4 the applicable aluminum section 232 duty also had priority under the revised stacking rule.
- **Reciprocal IEEPA:** no separate amount is shown. The Canadian border-order treatment and the section 232 exception limited its application, and CBP reports the IEEPA tariffs unlawful.
- **Section 122 surcharge:** not yet in effect for 2025; for March 2 and June 10, 2026, the full-value section 232 duty prevents an additional surcharge on this all-aluminum article; U also has the stated USMCA exception. The surcharge’s stated period had ended by the later entries.
- **Section 338:** not yet effective before August 22, 2026. On August 25 and September 14 the original motor-vehicles proclamation excepted articles subject to section 232; from September 15 the scope proclamation made the two duties cumulative.
- **Forced-labor section 301:** not yet effective before July 24, 2026. Thereafter the express aluminum/heading 9903.82.02 exemption applies in both cases; U also has the free-USMCA-entry exemption.

*For N on March 14 and June 3, $25,000 is the most defensible present-law reading, not a resolved refund calculation: with the IEEPA duty unlawful and liquidation directed without regard to it, the underlying section 232 duty remains.
