The following are text-based discrepancies or tensions, distinguished from ordinary prospective amendments:

1. Proposal's location of exemptions. The June notice says the proposed exemptions 'in Annex A' include all articles and parts currently subject to section 232 tariffs, and also describes informational materials and donations among proposed exemptions. But Annex A's readable introductory note says those categories are excluded 'in addition to the products listed in this Annex.' 2. Dairy scope reference. The dairy exclusion's operative clause (3) refers to a September 8 proclamation modifying the scope of the dairy duties. The published September 8 scope proclamations are for alcoholic beverages and motor vehicles, not dairy; no dairy scope proclamation was found through today (FR Doc. 2026-18836, 91 FR 58320; FR Docs. 2026-18838 and 2026-18839, 91 FR 58331, 58339).

3. Description of the 60 economies. The July action notice says all investigated economies 'have failed to impose a forced labor import prohibition and to effectively enforce such a prohibition,' and parenthetically says all failed 'both factors.' Its preceding list says six failed to effectively enforce; the June proposal expressly identifies those six as economies that impose a prohibition (FR Doc. 2026-15181, 91 FR 47318; FR Doc. 2026-11296, 91 FR 34274). Read literally as failure of each individual factor, the July sentence conflicts; read as failure to satisfy the combined two-factor test, it can be reconciled.

4. Printed start date after suspension. Original operative clauses and recitals print August 19, while the later suspension's operative clause sets August 22 and amends the Annex II chapeaux, not those printed clauses. Later proclamations also recount the original August 19 date before describing the suspension and August 22 start.
