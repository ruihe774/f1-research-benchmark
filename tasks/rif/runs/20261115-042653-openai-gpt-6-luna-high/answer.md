## Q14

**Answer:**
(i) **Preamble/text differences in OPM’s appeals rule.** First, the preamble says §351.901(c) makes the procedures in “part 351” the sole and exclusive means of appeal (2026-15666, 91 FR 49244). As corrected, §351.901(c)(1) says the procedures in “this subpart” are the exclusive means (2026-17338, 91 FR 54796 correcting 2026-15666, 91 FR 49263). Second, the section analysis says the appellant must prove that the appeal was timely “and in the required form” (2026-15666, 91 FR 49248); §351.901(b)(1) lists timeliness, but not a separate burden element of proper form, while §351.903(a) separately states the appeal’s form/content requirements (91 FR 49263–49264). Third, the preamble summary says public posting is limited to protect “protected personnel information” (91 FR 49231); the correction changes the regulatory phrase to “protected personal information” (§351.909(f), 2026-17338, 91 FR 54796 correcting 2026-15666, 91 FR 49267).

(ii) **Proposal-to-final differences not acknowledged in the summary of changes.** (1) The final/corrected §351.901(c)(1) changes “this part” in the proposal to “this subpart”; the summary still describes the exclusive process as part 351 procedures. (2) §351.902(a) changes the filing object from reconsideration of an “initial appeal” under “this part” to reconsideration of an “initial decision” under “this subpart”; the summary does not identify that correction. (3) §351.901(c)(3) omits the Federal Labor Relations Authority from the proposed list of collateral forums, while the summary lists the final forums but does not call out the deletion. (4) §351.903(b) changes the agency-response deadline from “within 30 calendar days of the initial appeal” to “within 30 calendar days after service,” with a separate rule that the 30 days begin upon service; the summary does not mention that trigger clarification. (5) §351.905(c) removes the proposal’s authority to direct “any other appropriate action” and retains the more specific fact-development tools; the summary discusses narrowing audits/investigations but does not mention deleting that authority. (6) §351.906(b) changes mandatory sanctions (“OPM shall”) to discretionary sanctions (“OPM may”), and final §351.906(c) adds that sanctions must be proportionate, causally related, and no broader than necessary; the summary’s protective-order discussion does not identify either change.

(iii) The MSPB rule has conflicting transition statements. Its DATES text says it will not apply the rule to pending or newly filed cases concerning agency actions taken before the effective date of OPM’s rulemakings and will continue to hear those RIF appeals (2026-16456, 91 FR 51997). Its RIF preamble says MSPB will accept appeals where the agency issued the RIF notice before September 2 (91 FR 51997). But its later “Effective Date of Amendment” section says MSPB will adjudicate RIF appeals received after September 2 only where the RIF effective date preceded September 2 (91 FR 51998). OPM’s appeals rule, in turn, makes its process apply by **notice issuance** on or after September 2 (2026-15666, 91 FR 49230, DATES). Thus a pre-September 2 notice with a post-September 2 effective date is treated differently by the MSPB preamble and its later effective-date statement.

OPM proposal: 2026-02576, 91 FR 5861–5877; OPM final rule: 2026-15666, 91 FR 49230–49267; OPM correction: 2026-17338, 91 FR 54796; MSPB final rule: 2026-16456, 91 FR 51997–51998. The corrections quoted above are part of the regulatory text as of November 14, 2026.

**Uncertainty:**
The MSPB transition inconsistency leaves the Board’s treatment of a pre-effective-date notice with a later effective separation unclear. The items in (i) are textual discrepancies, not conclusions about their legal effect.
