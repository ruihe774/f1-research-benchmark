**(a) Jordan (GS-13, one-grade interval; Level 2; no preference; career, so competitive service tenure subgroup I).** A released employee with a current annual rating of Level 2 (Minimally Satisfactory) or higher has assignment rights (351.701(a)). Rights reach positions (i) held by an employee with **lower retention standing in the same tenure group** (351.701(b)(1)); (ii) **not more than three grades below** his position, measured by the released employee's own grade progression, which for a one-grade interval GS position means GS-12, GS-11 and GS-10 (351.701(b)(2), (d)(1)–(2)); (iii) in the **same competitive area** and with the **same type of work schedule** (351.701(a)); and (iv) for which he is qualified (351.701(b)(3)).
- **Available:** #1 (GS-12, Ellis, lower standing), #2 (GS-11, Frank, lower), #3 (GS-10, Gale, lower).
- **Not available:** #4 GS-9 (four grades below, outside the three-grade limit); #5 (Ito has higher standing, 351.701(b)(1)); #6 (part-time, a different work schedule, 351.701(a)); #7 (different competitive area, 351.701(a)).
- **What must be offered:** the position requiring "no reduction, or the least possible reduction, in representative rate," which is **#1, the GS-12 held by Ellis**.

**(b) If Jordan has a compensable service-connected disability of 30 percent or more:** the limit becomes **five grades** (351.701(b)(2)), so **#4 (GS-9, Hana)** also becomes available. The required offer does not change: the agency must still offer the **GS-12 (#1)**, the least possible reduction.

**(c) If his current annual rating of record is Level 1:** 351.701(a) applies only to an employee with a current annual rating of "minimally satisfactory (Level 2) or equivalent, or higher." A Level 1 employee has **no assignment rights** to any of the seven positions; the agency furloughs or separates him under subpart F.

**(d) If Jordan and all seven holders are in the excepted service:** 351.701(a) covers "a competitive service tenure group employee," so **no assignment rights arise** under part 351. Under **351.705(a)** an agency may, at its discretion, adopt provisions giving excepted-service competing employees assignment rights to positions under the same appointing authority "on the same basis as" 351.701; absent such a provision, none of the positions is available and Jordan is released.

**(e) The old handbook's two ideas.** The 2026 preamble (91 FR 49203) says the rule consolidates former 351.701(b) and (c) and eliminates "the separate concepts of an employee 'bumping' another employee in a lower subgroup, and 'retreating' to the position of a lower-ranked employee in the same subgroup." Both are replaced by a single right: assignment to a position held by another employee with lower retention standing in the same tenure group, not more than three (or five) grades below, for which the employee is qualified (351.701(b)). The preamble also states that OPM is eliminating former **351.701(d)**: the old restriction on employees with a Level 2 rating "from obtaining assignment to positions held by employees with a higher performance rating ... is no longer necessary." So the handbook's rule that a Level 2 employee cannot take a position held by someone with a higher rating is no longer the rule; what controls is the comparison of retention standing, in which ratings appear only as performance credit.

**(b) OPM hears it; she must file through OPM's electronic filing system; the last day is Tuesday, January 19, 2027 at 11:59 p.m. ET.** Under 351.901(a) an employee "separated ... by a reduction-in-force action taken under this part may appeal that action to OPM." Filing is exclusively through "the electronic filing system identified on OPM's website"; e-mail or postal filings are not accepted absent good cause and OPM approval (351.902(a), (c)). The deadline is within 30 calendar days from the effective date of the action, timely if e-filed by 11:59 p.m. Eastern Time on the 30th calendar day (351.902(b)); the first day counted is the day after the effective date, and if the last day falls on a Saturday, Sunday or Federal holiday the period extends to the first workday (351.902(b)(1)). Counting: effective date **Friday, Dec. 18, 2026**, so day 1 = Dec. 19; Dec. 19–31 = days 1–13; Jan. 1, 2027 = day 14; **Jan. 17, 2027 = day 30**, a Sunday, so the next workday is **Monday, Jan. 18, 2027**, which is Martin Luther King, Jr. Day and a Federal holiday. The period therefore runs to **Tuesday, January 19, 2027**, 11:59 p.m. ET.

**(c) What she must prove; hearing.** By a preponderance of the evidence she must prove (1) the timeliness of the written appeal; (2) OPM jurisdiction; (3) that the agency failed to comply with an applicable statute or OPM regulation governing RIF actions under part 351; and (4) that the failure **prejudiced** her by causing the appealed action or the loss of a materially more favorable outcome (351.901(b)). She is **not entitled to a hearing**: review "must be based solely on the developed written record unless OPM determines that a hearing is necessary and efficient," defined as an insufficiently developed record or a material disputed issue of witness credibility (351.905(c)).

**(d) The union cannot use the negotiated grievance procedure.** 351.901(c)(1)–(2): the subpart I procedures are the sole and exclusive means of appealing a RIF action, and a RIF action taken under part 351 "and any matter relating to an appeal of such an action, may not be raised under a negotiated grievance procedure or contested through grievance arbitration." Between GS-11 (Hill) and GS-10 (Jones), the rule requires "no reduction, or the least possible reduction, in representative rate," so **must be offered: Hill's GS-11.**
- **Dube (18.0; subgroup II; no disability, so the three-grade limit applies).** Dube's 18.0 exceeds every holder's total (Gomez 17.0, Jones 15.0, Hill 14.0, Ikeda 9.0); the tenure subgroup matters only as a tie-breaker (351.501(c)), so all four have lower retention standing. GS-9 is outside the limit; the least possible reduction is at GS-12, so **must be offered: Gomez's GS-12.**

**(d) If every offer is accepted: Gomez, Hill and Ikeda are separated.** Brandt moves into the GS-9, Estrada into the GS-11 and Dube into the GS-12 (each retaining the same status and tenure, 351.701(a)). The three displaced incumbents are released from their competitive levels, and the scenario states they are qualified only for the position they hold, so they have no assignment rights and are separated. Alvarez, Chen and Farouk remain in GS-13 positions, Quinn remains (not a competing employee), and Jones remains in the GS-10. **The employees separated from the agency are Gomez, Hill and Ikeda.**

**Answer:**
Places where the preamble of 2026-15665 differs from the amendatory instructions or regulatory text printed in the same document:

1. **Erosion-of-duties reclassification: wrong paragraph and an extra "180 days" trigger.** Preamble (91 FR 49190): "OPM is modifying **Sec. 351.201(a)(2)** to remove from the list of actions that require agencies to use RIF procedures reclassification of an employee's position due to erosion of duties if the action would take effect after an agency had formally announced a RIF ... **and the RIF would take effect within 180 days**." The text: 351.201(a)(2) as revised (91 FR 49218) contains no erosion-of-duties language at all; the operative provision is **351.202(c)(3)** (91 FR 49218), which bars the reclassification "between the time an agency has formally announced a reduction in force in the employee's competitive area and the completion of the reduction in force where the reclassification action would adversely affect an employee's retention standing" — **no 180-day element**. **Another passage states it correctly:** 91 FR 49190 ("this rule will bar agencies from undertaking an erosion-of-duties reclassification between the formal announcement and completion of a RIF if the action would adversely affect an employee's retention standing").

2. **Competitive-area submission: "for OPM review" vs "approval in advance."** Preamble (91 FR 49179, summary of changes from the proposal): "an agency must submit a description of the competitive area and provide, **for OPM review**, a written explanation of the bona fide organizational basis for the competitive area." Regulatory text, 351.402(c) (91 FR 49220): the agency "shall submit a description of the competitive area, along with a written statement explaining the bona fide organizational basis for the competitive area, **to OPM for approval in advance of the reduction in force**." **Another passage is correct:** 91 FR 49203 ("the requirement in Sec. 351.402(c) that OPM **approve** new or substantially modified competitive areas that have been in effect less than 90 days prior to the issuance of RIF notices").

3. **Employees excluded "without using RIF procedures" vs a required modified notice.** Executive Summary (91 FR 49178): employees serving initial probationary periods, trial periods, temporary or time-limited appointments of one year or less, and Schedule C/G employees "are not 'competing employees' for RIF purposes and can be retained, furloughed, separated, demoted, or reassigned **without using RIF procedures**." Regulatory text, 351.202(d)(4) (91 FR 49218): if such an employee is furloughed for more than 30 days, separated, demoted or reassigned for a 351.201(a)(2) reason (and the action is not merely the expiration of a temporary or time-limited appointment), "the agency **must provide written notice under Sec. 351.801**," including specified content and a statement that the employee is not a competing employee and was not ranked. **Another passage states it correctly:** 91 FR 49179 (summary item 4) and 91 FR 49190–49191 ("the revised text clarifies that these excluded employees are still subject to the notice provisions of Sec. 351.801").

4. **Tenure subgroup II: "temporary or term appointment" vs "temporary appointment ... under subpart D."** Preamble (91 FR 49196): "Competitive service tenure subgroup II will consist of every other competitive service employee who, as of the date of the RIF notice, is not serving an initial probationary period or a **temporary or term appointment of 1 year or less**"; the preceding paragraph (91 FR 49196) says the tenure group excludes those serving "an initial probationary period or a **temporary or time-limited appointment of 1 year or less under 5 CFR part 316**." Regulatory text, 351.502(a)(3) (91 FR 49220): subgroup II includes employees not in subgroup I who are "not serving an initial probationary period or a **temporary appointment of 1 year or less under subpart D of part 316**" — no term appointments, no time-limited appointments, and only subpart D. **Another passage states it correctly:** 91 FR 49190 ("a temporary appointment of 1 year or less under subpart D of 5 CFR part 316") and 91 FR 49190–49191 (the final rule "removes a proposed reference to term appointments under part 316 subpart C").

5. **Missing ratings: where the "modal rating" comes from.** Preamble (91 FR 49198): paragraph (f) of 351.503 "uses the modal rating concept for employees with no ratings during the 4-year period prior to the RIF, **as currently found in Sec. 351.504(c)(1)**." Regulatory text, 351.503(f)(1) (91 FR 49221): credit "must be based on the modal rating **as defined in Sec. 351.203** for the summary level pattern that applies...." The two passages point to different sources, and the amendatory instructions add no "modal rating" definition to 351.203. A later passage (91 FR 49203) refers only to "use of the modal rating," citing neither, so the preamble nowhere states the point consistently with the text.

6. **Use of ratings: "may be considered" vs "must be based on."** Preamble (91 FR 49197): "Generally, the three most recent ratings of record received during the 4-year period prior to the date of issuance of reduction in force notices **may be considered**; however, an agency may establish a cut-off date...." Regulatory text, 351.503(c)(1) (91 FR 49220): "A competing employee's entitlement to performance consideration under this subpart **must be based on** the employee's three most recent summary level ratings of record received during the 4-year period...." **Another passage states it correctly:** 91 FR 49197 ("Agencies will list competing employees on the retention register ... based on each employee's performance credit, which is the sum of the values assigned for their three most recent ratings of record").

## Q14

**Answer:**
**A. (i) Preamble statements in OPM's appeals rule that differ from its regulatory text (as published or as corrected).**

1. **"Part 351" vs "this subpart."** The preamble says the final text provides that **part 351 procedures are the sole and exclusive means of appealing a RIF action** — 91 FR 49230 (digest, "Revising exclusivity language"), 91 FR 49243 ("Final Sec. 351.901(c) states that the procedures in **part 351** are the sole and exclusive means"), 91 FR 49244, 91 FR 49248 ("Paragraph (c)(1) provides that the procedures in **part 351** are the sole and exclusive means"), and 91 FR 49257. As **published**, the text read "The procedures in **this part** are the sole and exclusive means ..." (91 FR 49263), matching the preamble; the **correction** changed it to "The procedures in **this subpart** are the sole and exclusive means of appealing a reduction-in-force action taken under this part" (2026-17338, 91 FR 54796, item 1). The preamble was not conformed, so as corrected the rule's exclusivity reaches only subpart I procedures, while the preamble describes part 351 as the exclusive vehicle.

2. **Burden of proof: "timely and in the required form" vs "timeliness."** Preamble (91 FR 49248): "The appellant must prove, by a preponderance of the evidence, that the written appeal was **timely and in the required form**; that OPM has jurisdiction ...; that the agency failed to comply ...; and that the failure prejudiced the appellant." Regulatory text, 351.901(b)(1) (91 FR 49263, unchanged by the correction), requires proof only of "[t]he **timeliness** of the written appeal." The text contains no required-form element (form and content appear in 351.903 without a burden allocation), and no other preamble passage states the burden without it.

3. **The undefined term in 351.904(c).** Preamble (91 FR 49231): "**OPM or the responsible agency** may disallow an employee's choice of representative only where the representative is an employee of the responsible agency or OPM...." Text as corrected (2026-17338, 91 FR 54796, item 3): "**OPM or the agency whose action is under appeal** may ... disallow an employee's choice of representative when the representative is an employee of **that agency** or OPM...." OPM's correction notes that "responsible agency" is "undefined in part 351."

**B. (ii) Changes from the proposed appeals rule to the final rule that the final rule's summary of changes from the proposal does not acknowledge** (summary at 91 FR 49230–49232; proposed text at 91 FR 5874–5877).

1. **FLRA removed from the list of preserved forums.** Proposed 351.901(c) (91 FR 5874): the exclusive procedure does not preclude a complaint, appeal or other matter within the jurisdiction of EEOC, **the Federal Labor Relations Authority**, an Inspector General, MSPB, DOL VETS, or OSC. Final 351.901(c)(3) (91 FR 49263–49264): the list is EEOC, an Inspector General, MSPB, DOL VETS, and OSC — **FLRA is gone**, and a new bar on grievance procedures and arbitration appears instead. The digest's "Revising exclusivity language" paragraph (91 FR 49230) describes the grievance bar and the preserved forums but never flags the removal of FLRA.
2. **New exemption route in the e-filing registration requirement.** Proposed 351.902(c)(2) (91 FR 5875): "No party may electronically file any document with OPM or access an appeal or reconsideration of an appeal unless registered as an e-filer." Final 351.902(c)(2) (91 FR 49264): "... unless registered as an e-filer **or exempted under paragraph (c)(7) of this section for good cause**." The digest's e-filing paragraphs (91 FR 49230–49231) discuss narrowed enforcement and the withdrawal rule but not this carve-out.
3. **Hearing provision narrowed; "any other appropriate action" dropped.** Proposed 351.905(c) (91 FR 5876): review is record-based "unless OPM determines that a hearing **or any other appropriate action** is necessary and efficient to resolve an appeal **and directs the parties to participate in such hearing or comply with such action**." Final 351.905(c) (91 FR 49265): "... unless OPM determines that a **hearing** is necessary and efficient in resolving an appeal." The digest (91 FR 49231, "Tightening audits and investigations") addresses the audit/investigation standard but not the deletion of "any other appropriate action" or of the direction to the parties.
4. **Sanctions made discretionary.** Proposed 351.906(b) (91 FR 5876): when a party fails to comply with a directive, OPM "**shall**, except when prohibited by law," draw adverse inferences, prohibit evidence, or eliminate submissions. Final 351.906(b) (91 FR 49266): OPM "**may**, except when prohibited by law: ...." The digest's "Refining protective-order authority" paragraph (91 FR 49231) does not mention the change from "shall" to "may."
5. **New proportionality limit on sanctions.** Final 351.906(c) (91 FR 49266) adds: "Any sanction issued under paragraph (b) of this section must be proportionate, causally related to the violation, and no broader than necessary to protect the adjudicatory process." The proposed rule had no such paragraph and the digest does not acknowledge its addition.

**C. (iii) Inconsistencies about which RIF appeals the Board will still hear.**

1. **Within MSPB's rule: "action taken"/ "notice issued" vs "RIF effective date."** MSPB's DATES (91 FR 51997) say it "will not apply this rule to pending cases or to newly filed cases relating to **agency actions taken before the effective date** of OPM's rulemakings, and will continue to adjudicate such ... RIF appeals"; and its text (91 FR 51997) says MSPB "will continue to adjudicate RIF appeals that are not covered by OPM's final RIF appeals rule (i.e., because the RIF actions were taken before the rule's effective date), and will accept such cases for filing **where the agency's RIF notice was issued before the rule's effective date**." But Section III, "Effective Date of Amendment" (91 FR 51998), says: "MSPB will adjudicate **any RIF appeals it receives after September 2, 2026, in which the RIF effective date preceded September 2, 2026**." Three different triggers — date the action was taken, date the RIF notice was issued, and the RIF's own effective date — yield different answers whenever a notice issues before Sept. 2 but the separation takes effect after it (as in Q7(a): notice Aug. 31, 2026, separation Oct. 30, 2026).
2. **Between the Board and OPM: the operative trigger.** OPM's rule turns on the **date the agency issues the specific RIF notice**: it "applies only to a RIF action for which an agency issues the employee a specific RIF notice under 5 CFR 351.802 on or after September 2, 2026" (2026-15666 DATES, 91 FR 49230; preamble 91 FR 49262). MSPB's Section III turns on the **RIF effective date**, so for the same employee the Board's Section III and OPM's DATES can point in opposite directions.
3. **Between the Board and OPM: retained jurisdiction vs OPM's exclusivity claim.** OPM's preamble calls part 351 the "sole and exclusive means" of appealing a RIF action (91 FR 49230, 49243, 49244, 49248, 49257) and the corrected text calls subpart I that means (91 FR 54796), while MSPB expressly **retains** RIF appeal jurisdiction for one category — Foreign Service RIF actions under 22 U.S.C. 4010a — in redesignated 5 CFR 1201.3(a)(5) (91 FR 51998). OPM's 351.901(c)(3) preserves matters within the independent jurisdiction of MSPB (91 FR 49264), which reconciles the two; but the OPM text contains **no applicability limitation at all** (OPM says so at 91 FR 49248: "the final regulatory text accordingly does not include a separate applicability note in Sec. 351.901"), so an employee whose notice issued Aug. 31, 2026 is directed by OPM's preamble to MSPB and by MSPB's Section III (if the separation took effect after Sept. 2) elsewhere.

**Basis:**
2026-15666: digest 91 FR 49230–49232; preamble 91 FR 49243–49248, 49257, 49262; regulatory text 91 FR 49263–49267 (351.901–351.909). 2026-02576 (proposed appeals rule): 91 FR 5874–5877 (351.901, 351.902(c)(2), 351.904, 351.905(c), 351.906(b)). 2026-17338 (correction): 91 FR 54796. 2026-16456 (MSPB): 91 FR 51997–51998.

**Uncertainty:**
Item A.1 depends on the correction: before the Sept. 2, 2026 effective date the preamble and the text agreed (both "this part"), and after the correction the preamble is out of step. Item A.3 is a terminology difference that OPM itself described as non-substantive. For part C, the Board's three formulations cannot all be applied at once, and neither the Board's rule nor OPM's states a tie-breaking rule; how MSPB will docket a case with a pre-Sept. 2 notice and a post-Sept. 2 effective date is not resolved by the documents.
