(ii) Proposal-to-final changes not clearly acknowledged in the final rule’s change summary include: §351.902(a) changes reconsideration “of an initial appeal” to reconsideration “of an initial decision” (proposal 2026-02576, 91 FR 5874, 1936–41; final 2026-15666, 91 FR 49264, 4302–07); §351.902(c)(2) adds an exception allowing filing/access by a person exempted from e-filer registration under (c)(7), absent from proposal (proposal 91 FR 5875, 1961–65; final 91 FR 49264, 4334–42); §351.905(c) deletes the proposal’s express power to require “any other appropriate action” in addition to a hearing, replacing it with the narrower hearing language and separate information/audit authority (proposal 91 FR 5876, 2091–2103; final 91 FR 49265–49266, 4532–58); §351.906(b) changes mandatory “shall” sanctions to discretionary “may” (proposal 91 FR 5877, 2145–54; final 91 FR 49266, 4590–99); and final §351.906(c) adds a sanction proportionality/causal-relation limit (91 FR 49266, 4600–02), not identified in the summary. The final also omits the proposal’s express statement that the OPM process “shall supersede” agency-policy and CBA appeal procedures; the summary describes exclusivity and the grievance bar but does not identify that deletion (proposal 91 FR 5875, 1922–31; final 91 FR 49263–49264, 4279–95). (iii) The Board’s rule retains only Foreign Service RIF appeals in its regulatory text, while its preamble says it will still hear legacy pending/new non-Foreign-Service RIF appeals (2026-16456, 91 FR 51998, 115–40, 180–87; regulatory text, 91 FR 51998, 269–99). The transition triggers also conflict: Board preamble says in one passage it will accept post-September 2 cases where the RIF notice was issued before that date (91 FR 51998, 130–40), but elsewhere says it will hear new cases only where the RIF effective date preceded September 2 (91 FR 51998, 180–84). OPM’s rule uses notice issuance—not effective date—as the dividing line (2026-15666, 91 FR 49230, 83–85).

**Basis:**
OPM proposed rule 2026-02576, 91 FR 5861–5877; OPM final rule 2026-15666, 91 FR 49230–49267, corrected by 2026-17338, 91 FR 54796; Board rule 2026-16456, 91 FR 51997–51998.

**Uncertainty:**
The CBA tension is between operative text and the preamble’s treatment of existing agreements; the Federal Register does not resolve how a particular existing agreement would be treated. The Board’s transition passages state competing criteria, and the regulatory text itself does not codify the legacy-case exception.
