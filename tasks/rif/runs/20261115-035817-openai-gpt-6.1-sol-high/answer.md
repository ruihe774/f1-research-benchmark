6. **§351.905(c), additional-information power and other-action language.** P's ascertainment paragraph authorizes audits/investigations and a hearing **‘or any other appropriate action’**, including a direction to participate or comply (**5876**). F expressly adds power to **require either party to provide additional information**, and its written-record exception refers to a **hearing**, omitting the proposal's ‘any other appropriate action’ formulation (**49265–49266**). The opening summary addresses the tighter audit standard and representative participation but does not identify these textual changes (**49231**).

7. **§351.906(b), mandatory versus discretionary sanctions.** P says OPM **‘shall’** impose one of the listed consequences for failure to comply with the directive (**5876**). F says OPM **‘may’** do so (**49266**). The opening summary addresses protective-order scope and removal of a separate ascertainment sanction, but not this shall-to-may change.

8. **§351.906(c), new limits on sanctions.** F adds that a sanction must be **proportionate, causally related to the violation, and no broader than necessary** (**49266**); P has no corresponding paragraph (**5876**). The opening summary discusses limits on protective orders, not these separate limits on sanctions. The latter are acknowledged later in the preamble (**49251**).

9. **§351.909(c), accepted untimely reconsideration.** F expressly states that, if OPM **accepts and grants an untimely request for good cause**, the resulting reconsidered decision becomes final under (b) (**49266**). P's finality section has no corresponding rule (**5877**). The opening summary explains that an untimely request does not suspend finality, but does not identify this additional accepted-and-granted-request provision (**49232**).

(c) **Kind (iii): MSPB transition inconsistencies.** B = **2026-16456**, *Appellate Jurisdiction Update II*.

1. **Notice date versus action effective date.** B's section I.B correctly reports OPM's specific-notice trigger and expressly says MSPB will accept later-filed cases **where the agency's notice was issued before the effective date** (**91 FR 51997**). But B's DATES statement speaks of agency actions **taken before** OPM's effective date (**51997**), and section III specifies later-filed RIF appeals **whose RIF effective date preceded September 2** (**51998**). OPM's F, DATES (**49230**) and applicability discussion (**49248**), instead preserve MSPB review for **pre-September 2 notices**, including notices whose resulting separations occur later. These formulations differ for an August notice/October separation.

2. **Effective on September 2 versus immediately on publication.** B's DATES statement and section III make the Board amendment effective **September 2, 2026** (**51997–51998**). Its APA discussion nevertheless says there is good cause to make the final rule effective **immediately upon publication** (**51998**), which was **August 12**. That is inconsistent effective-date language, potentially affecting the purported jurisdictional transition during August 12–September 1. The formal DATES statement and section III supply the September 2 date; the isolated APA sentence does not reconcile itself with them.
