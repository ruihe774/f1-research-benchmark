**Answer:**

Final 8 CFR 214.2(f)(5)(ii)(A) and (f)(8)(i)(C) still bar graduate-level transfers during a program, but now allow an SEVP-authorized exception for extenuating circumstances such as school closure or prolonged inability to hold in-person classes; DHS states the NPRM proposed prohibiting graduate transfers without exception and that it amended the text. Final 8 CFR 214.2(f)(5)(ii)(C) applies to an alien who 'has completed a program after September 15, 2026'; DHS explains programs completed before the effective date are not counted and gives the example that a pre-effective-date master's graduate may do another master's. See 91 FR 45097, 45104 & n.321, 45125. (3) The delay/suspension safety valve was time-limited and given a Federal Register notice requirement. Final 8 CFR 214.2(f)(5)(ii)(E) allows delay/suspension of (f)(5)(ii)(A)-(C) only until September 14, 2028, requires announcement by Federal Register notification and SEVP's website, and requires implementation dates to be announced on SEVP's website at least 30 days in advance. See 91 FR 45097, 45104-45105, 45125. (4) Early-ending students' duties were clarified. Final 8 CFR 214.2(f)(5)(v) adds that a student who ends study/training before the admission period expires must, with eligible dependents, depart or otherwise seek to maintain lawful status within 30 days from the study/training end date, and that a student who fails to maintain a full course/status gets no departure period and must leave immediately. See 91 FR 45097, 45105, 45125. (5) Post-completion OPT status route was clarified to include CBP admission after travel as an alternative to USCIS EOS. Final 8 CFR 214.2(f)(10)(ii)(D) states that, unless within 214.1(m)(1)(i), a post-completion OPT student must apply for employment authorization and either apply for EOS or seek admission through CBP after travel abroad. See 91 FR 45097, 45127. (6) The OPT start-date window was conformed in the final rule. Final amendment changed 8 CFR 214.2(f)(11)(i)(D) from 60 to 30 days for requesting an OPT start date after program end, aligning with the 30-day filing change in (f)(11)(i)(B)(2); DHS says it overlooked this update in the NPRM and made it in the final rule.
